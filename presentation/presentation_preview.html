1. Motivation: The Prefetching Dilemma in Shared LLCs

Background
The Hardware Prefetching Dilemma
  • Latency Hiding: Hardware prefetchers (AMPM, IP-Stride, SPP) successfully hide DRAM latency for regular stride / stream patterns (403.gcc, 470.lbm).
  • Accuracy Collapse: For irregular and pointer-chasing patterns (429.mcf), prefetch accuracy collapses to < 0.5%.
  • Cache Pollution: Speculatively loaded cache lines flood the Last-Level Cache without ever receiving a hit.
Multicore Thrashing & Policy Blindness
  • Prefetch-Oblivious Policies: Standard LRU and SRRIP treat speculative prefetches with the same high priority as demand fills.
  • Cross-Core Eviction: In a shared 4-core LLC, inaccurate prefetches from one core (e.g. mcf) prematurely evict active working-set data of other cores.
  • IPC Degradation: Causes severe performance collapse (up to 50%+ IPC degradation under contention).

2. Literature Review & Critical Research Gaps

Prior Art
State-of-the-Art Approaches
  • Drishti (MICRO 2025): Addresses LLC slicing using per-core GPHT tables and sampled cache selection. Author clarification: Drishti is an architectural framework, not a standalone replacement policy; replacement policies must handle prefetch vs. demand.
  • PACMan (IEEE CAL 2023): Proposes accuracy-based prefetch management; relies on dedicated sampling sets and feedback counters.
  • DRRIP (ISCA 2010): Set-dueling dynamic RRIP using 10-bit PSEL saturating counters.
  • SHiP (ISCA 2011): Signature-based hit predictor requiring an 8,192-entry SHCT table (2,048 Bytes = 2 KB of dedicated SRAM!).
Critical Research Gaps Identified
  • Heavy Storage Overhead: Existing prefetch-aware or predictive policies require thousands of bits of dedicated SRAM tables or counter logic.
  • Complexity & Latency: Training periods, hash tables, and set-dueling logic introduce pipeline area and energy penalties.
  • Our Research Goal: Can we achieve superior prefetch resilience with TRUE ZERO hardware storage overhead (0 extra metadata bits) across heterogeneous multicore workloads?

3. Proposed Policy Architecture: Demoted Insertion & Hit Promotion

Core Mechanism
Three Architectural Principles
  • 1. Demoted Initial Insertion:
    • Demand fills $\rightarrow$ Inserted at high priority (MRU / $RRPV=2$).
    • Speculative Prefetch fills $\rightarrow$ Demoted to distant priority (near-LRU / $RRPV=3$).
    • Result: Useless prefetches sit in the victim position and exit without displacing active demand data.
  • 2. Self-Adaptive Hit Promotion:
    • On cache hit, prefetched line is immediately promoted to MRU / $RRPV=0$.
    • Line is reclassified as verified demand data (shielded from eviction).
  • 3. PA-Adaptive Dynamic Extension (0.75 Bytes):
    • Lightweight 6-bit saturating counter (+3 on prefetch hit, -1 on unreferenced prefetch eviction).
    • Automatically toggles insertion between $RRPV=2$ (streaming) and $RRPV=3$ (noise filtering).

Automatic Filtering: Useful prefetches hit and promote (>50% in gcc), while pointer noise (<0.5% in mcf) is safely pruned.

4. ChampSim 4-Core Heterogeneous Workload Results

Evaluation
Key Performance Breakthroughs (20M Sim)
  • CPU 0 (gcc - Mixed Stride): IPC jumps from 0.4248 to 2.1800 (5.13x Speedup / +413% Boost) because useful prefetches are promoted to MRU and protected.
  • CPU 1 (mcf - Pointer Chasing): Low-priority insertion demotes useless pointer prefetches, boosting IPC from 0.0743 to 0.0993 (+33.6%).
  • CPU 2 (perlbench - Compute-Bound): High cache locality preserved without cross-core interference; IPC stable at 2.019.
  • CPU 3 (omnetpp - Graph Traversal): Cross-core thrashing suppressed; IPC increases from 0.2566 to 0.3207 (+25.0%).
